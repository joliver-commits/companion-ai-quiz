How well do you know your AI?

Everyone's arguing about whether AI companions are ruining young people. Nobody can agree on what an AI companion even is. Is it a special app — or is it just whatever you're already doing with the chatbot on your phone?

This quiz takes the second view. It ignores what your app calls itself and looks at what you actually do with it, scoring you on the four Companion Index features researchers use to define companionship: memory, emotional responsiveness, human-likeness and constant availability.

Where do I paste this?

ChatGPT: Settings → Personalization → Custom instructions → "Anything else ChatGPT should know."

Claude: Settings → Profile → personal preferences, or a Project's custom instructions.

Gemini: Settings → Saved info.

Copilot / Meta AI / Character.AI: varies — look for "personalisation", "custom instructions", "persona" or "memory". If there's nowhere to put it, paste it as your first message in a new chat; it'll hold for that conversation.

The hub publishes these in the Agent Skills format, which works with Claude (Desktop and Code), OpenAI Codex and Custom GPTs, and the Gemini CLI — there they trigger automatically instead of needing to be pasted.

Honest heads-up, and the hub says this too: a skill is guidance an agent may follow, not a hard constraint. Models drift, especially in long conversations. For safety-critical things — crisis routing above all — this is a starting point, not a guarantee. If it slides back into old habits, paste it again. That's normal, not you doing it wrong.

What is this quiz actually measuring?

Four design features, taken from Sun, Wang & McDaniel's definition of companion AI in Child Development Perspectives: persistent memory, emotional responsiveness, anthropomorphism and constant availability. It's the only definition in the literature you can apply to a product without first asking the company what kind of product it is.

Most definitions of "AI companion" start from the product — is this a companion app or an assistant? The trouble is that the four features show up across both. A dedicated companion app switches all four on by default. A general assistant switches them on depending on how you use it. So the same app can be a homework tool for one person and a confidant for another, and no product-based rule can tell the difference.

That's why this quiz scores your usage, not your app. Illinois wrote the same idea into law in S.B. 3262, defining a companion AI product by what it does "irrespective of how the system is marketed or labeled" — then presuming a product is one if it "retains memory of past conversations with a specific user to inform future responses."

The inversion worth knowing: 78% of AI-using minors use ChatGPT, which sits outside every companion-specific rule. Character.AI, at 15%, is the most-used tool those rules do reach — and it's a product that disavows the companion label in its own marketing copy. Replika, CHAI, Chub AI and Nomi, the products the category was written around, sit at 2% or below.

Honest limits: this is a magazine quiz, not an instrument. It isn't validated, the thresholds are chosen rather than derived, and it can't tell you whether your usage is healthy — only what shape it has. A high score is not a diagnosis and a low score is not a gold star. Thorn's own data also can't distinguish a minor who asked ChatGPT for homework help from one who confided in it nightly, which is exactly the gap this quiz is a toy model of.
